## Overview

This paper evaluates whether large language models can infer aggregate neighborhood-level mobility outcomes from urban context, and whether those predictions align with empirically observed context–mobility relationships.

## Key Findings

- Supervised baselines outperform zero-shot LLMs on Census Block Group–level mobility prediction.
- LLMs can partially recover aggregate mobility patterns from neighborhood context.
- Directional analysis shows LLMs rely on coarse, stable priors that often stay invariant across outcomes and cities.
